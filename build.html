🦠 Project: Build a Virus

Beginner-friendly · About 30–40 minutes · Best after Mission 1

A virus isn't even made of cells — it's a tiny package with three key parts, and that's exactly what your model needs to show.

Materials

  • 1 round base — a polystyrene ball, a scrunched-up ball of newspaper wrapped in tape, or a small balloon
  • Colored paper, felt, or paint to cover the base
  • A short length of wool, string, or pipe cleaner (for the genetic material)
  • 4–8 toothpicks or short pipe cleaners, plus small paper triangles or beads (for spike proteins)
  • Glue, tape, scissors, marker pen, small labels or sticky notes
Need a refresher? Rewatch the "Viruses (Updated)" video in Mission 1 if you're not sure what any of these parts actually do.

🧫 Project: Phagocyte in Action

Intermediate · About 40–50 minutes · Best after Mission 3

This one's a diorama, not just a single model — it should show the exact moment a phagocyte engulfs a pathogen.

Materials

  • 1 larger ball or a partly-deflated balloon (the phagocyte)
  • Modeling clay or play-dough (to reshape it and add texture)
  • 1 small ball or bead, a different color (the pathogen)
  • A few small dots or beads in a third color (digestive enzymes)
  • A cardboard base, glue, marker pen, small labels
Need a refresher? Revisit the phagocytosis steps in Mission 3 before you start shaping your blob.

🔑 Project: Antibody & Antigen (Lock and Key)

Intermediate · About 30–40 minutes · Best after Mission 4

The whole point of this model is showing that an antibody fits ONE antigen — and nothing else.

Materials

  • 2 pipe cleaners (for the antibody's Y-shape)
  • 2 buttons or beads with clearly different shapes/sizes (the antigens)
  • Cardboard, colored paper, glue, scissors, marker pen
Need a refresher? Re-read the "lock and key" explanation in Mission 4 if you get stuck on why the second button shouldn't fit.
